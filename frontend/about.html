DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

What is Task Atlas?

Task Atlas is a map for choosing what Physical AI data to collect. Start from an object, scene, state, intent, skill, dataset, or robot; follow connected task candidates; inspect what is known and what still needs a trial.

Explore broadly · Ground every claim · Keep unknowns visible

The principles behind the map

01

Begin anywhere

Objects, scenes, states, intents, skills, datasets, and robots are different starting points. Typed relationships connect them, so exploration can follow the question at hand instead of forcing every task into one fixed tree.

02

Define tasks by their goals

A task describes a meaningful change from an initial state to a goal. Procedures are optional proposals for reaching that goal; skills describe capabilities, not a required choreography. Demonstrations remain separate records of observed action.

03

Keep uncertainty visible

An omitted field is unknown. “None” means someone checked and found nothing. A ready assessment means the declared planning requirements are met; it does not mean a robot has succeeded.

04

Give every claim its own evidence

Task suitability, human frequency, asset compatibility, and robot execution answer different questions. Each source is tied to the claim and scope it can support. Dataset counts or video appearances are not treated as everyday population frequency.

05

Make the next trial reviewable

A collection card gathers the setup, reset, success checks, failure modes, required resources, and unresolved conditions for a candidate. A proposal stays a proposal until observations support a stronger claim.

06

Use each source within its limits

Benchmarks, object catalogs, activity surveys, and video datasets reveal different parts of the map. Their coverage and biases stay attached to the source; no single dataset defines all useful tasks.

FROM EXPLORATION TO COLLECTION

A grounded path from a question to a plan

Task Atlas uses object sets such as YCB as physical anchors, then connects concepts, situations, goals, and reusable skills. A selected candidate can become a collection plan while its assumptions, evidence, and unknowns remain inspectable.
