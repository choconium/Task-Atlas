GET STARTED

Find the right task before you collect data

Task Atlas starts from an everyday object and asks which tasks make sense in a given situation. It shows what is ready, what is missing, and which claims have sources, then turns a candidate into a collection card you can use for a simulation or teleoperation session.

About 5 minutes · no sign-in needed

Five steps

  1. 1

    Choose an object

    Pick an object from the Objects list on the left, or type a name into the search box at the top. The mustard bottle is selected when the page opens, so you can start with it.

  2. 2

    Describe the situation

    In Context, set the Scene, Contents, Role, and Asset profile. Leave a field unspecified when you do not know it; choose “None” only when you have checked and found none. Open “Resources, capabilities, and confirmed conditions” to declare what is present.

  3. 3

    Compare candidate tasks

    Candidates update as you change the context. Each one is labelled ready, needs changes, unknown, or blocked. Explore keeps every candidate and says what would change its assessment; Ready only hides the rest.

  4. 4

    Inspect a task and download its card

    Click a candidate to open it in the Inspector. It shows the goal and success criteria, initial state, required capabilities, alternative procedures, and each claim with its source and limits. Choose a procedure, then press “Download collection card” to save the plan as JSON.

  5. 5

    Follow the relationships

    In the Relationship graph, click any node (a scene, state, intent, skill, or task) to put it at the centre. Use the Context, Goals, and Execution lenses to narrow the links, and Back to return.

TRY THIS FIRST

See context change the answer

With the mustard bottle selected, change one field at a time and watch the candidates:

  • Scene “Home kitchen” and Contents “Full”: preparation and dispensing come forward.
  • Change Contents to “Empty”: checking, replacement, disposal, and cleaning take over.
  • Scene “Supermarket shelf”, then switch Role between “Customer” and “Staff”: purchase tasks give way to restocking, facing, and inventory work.
GOOD TO KNOW

How to read the results

Unknown is not “no”
Anything you have not declared stays unknown. The atlas does not fill it in for you.
Four separate claims
Whether a task is possible, how often it happens, whether an asset fits, and whether a robot can do it are recorded and sourced separately.
Procedures are options
A task is defined by its goal. Listed procedures are alternative ways to reach it, not a required sequence.
A card is a plan, not a result
A collection card prepares a trial. It does not claim that a robot has already succeeded.
